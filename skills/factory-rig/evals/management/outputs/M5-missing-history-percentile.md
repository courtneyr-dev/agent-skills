# Reply to the product director

**I can't give you an 85th percentile cycle time or a promisable date yet.** The team has finished 0 items since it started tracking this week. A percentile needs finished items; with zero, any number I gave you would be invented.

## What we know today

- Work in progress: 5 items.
- Completed: 0. Throughput and cycle time: no data.
- Work item age: we can report how long each of the 5 items has been in progress. That's the useful signal right now, and I'll send it.

## What I recommend telling the customer

Don't promise a date this week. If the customer needs something now, give a learning date: "We'll confirm a delivery window by <date>." If the team or an engineering lead has a judgment-based estimate, label it as an estimate with a range, not a forecast from data.

## How we get to a real forecast

1. Record start and finish dates for every item from now on.
2. As items finish, I'll share cycle times with the sample stated (for example, "3 items over 2 weeks"). Small samples give wide ranges.
3. About 10 to 12 weeks of history is the usual basis for a stable 85th percentile.
4. For multi-item delivery, I'll run a Monte Carlo forecast from weekly throughput and give a range with confidence levels.
5. Small, independently deliverable items tighten the forecast sooner.

## Assumptions

- "Delivery date" means delivery of one or more of the tracked items; the scope for the customer isn't stated.
- No usable history exists from before this week (for example, closed tickets with start and finish dates). If it does, I'll use it and say how comparable it is.
