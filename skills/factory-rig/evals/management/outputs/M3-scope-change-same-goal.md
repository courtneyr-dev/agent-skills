# Mid-sprint scope change, day 4 of 10

**Advice: don't cancel the sprint.** The Sprint Goal, "customers can pay with a saved card", hasn't become obsolete, and the Developers say they can still meet it. Scrum expects the Sprint Backlog to change during a sprint as long as the goal holds.

## Who decides what

- **Product Owner:** orders the Product Backlog and is the only person who can cancel a sprint. Cancellation fits when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. A changed item list doesn't meet that test.
- **Developers:** own the Sprint Backlog and the plan for meeting the goal. They've judged the swap workable; that's their call.
- **Stakeholder:** gives input to the Product Owner. Doesn't decide cancellation or the team's plan.
- **Release approval:** Sprint Review inspects the increment and adapts the backlog. It isn't a release gate. Releases go through this organization's release approver, and an increment can ship before Sprint Review.

## What I'll do as project manager

1. Record the swap: two items out, one larger item in, the reason, and day 4.
2. Ask the Developers about risk on the larger item. A smaller slice can still meet the goal; work short of the Definition of Done isn't done.
3. Report scope change separately from progress.
4. Check whether any outside commitment depended on the removed items. If so, its owner approves the change.
5. Tell the stakeholder their scope-churn concern is fair input for the Product Owner and Sprint Review.

## Assumptions

- The team follows the Scrum Guide (2020) roles and events.
- The new item serves the same Sprint Goal.
- No contract ties release approval to Sprint Review. If one does, that contract governs and I'll route it to its owner.
