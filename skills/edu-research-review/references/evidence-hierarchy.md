# Evidence types and what each can carry

Classify every source before you weigh a claim from it. The type limits the claim; a strong design in a weak venue still needs tracing to the full paper.

| Type | What it is | What it can support | What it cannot support alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | Protocol-driven search and synthesis of many studies | The state of evidence on a defined question, with its gaps | A claim about a setting or population the included studies did not sample |
| Peer-reviewed study | One study reviewed by a journal or refereed conference | Its own findings, within its sample, setting and measures | A general teaching rule; transfer to other levels without an argument |
| Preprint | Author-posted paper, possibly before review | A finding to check; note whether a reviewed version exists | Anything stated as settled |
| Conference talk or poster | Presentation, often without full methods | A lead to the underlying paper | Any claim until traced to a paper and its methods |
| Association webpage or standards research summary | Organization-authored synthesis (for example CSTA's research page for its 2026 standards) | Where to look; what the association relied on | The cited studies' findings; read those papers |
| Practitioner guidance | Advice from experienced educators | Practical options to test | Effectiveness claims |

## Design and causal language

- Random assignment to a comparison group (RCT, matched pairs then random assignment) supports causal wording inside the sampled population and setting.
- A quasi-experiment with named confound controls supports cautious causal wording with the remaining threats stated. Without controls it supports none.
- Correlational, descriptive, case and qualitative designs support descriptions, associations, mechanisms and hypotheses, not causal claims.
- A non-significant difference is not evidence of no difference. Equivalence needs an equivalence test or a power analysis (Lakens 2017).
- A post hoc subgroup split is a hypothesis for a new study (Wang et al. 2007).
- Many outcomes with uncorrected tests inflate false positives (Simmons, Nelson, Simonsohn 2011).

## Transfer across levels and settings

Research on higher-education computer science courses does not establish effectiveness in K-12 BCIT classrooms or workplace learning. Each move across level (university to secondary), subject (programming to spreadsheets or accounting), setting (camp or lab to classroom) or population (volunteers to a whole class) needs a transfer argument: why the mechanism should hold, which conditions differ, and what local evidence would confirm it (Tipton and Olsen 2018). Without that argument, write the claim as a hypothesis to test.
