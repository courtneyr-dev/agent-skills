# Methods checklist for review, design and program evaluation

Use the same questions whether you critique someone else's study or draft your own. Record the section of the paper or plan that answers each one; "not reported" is an answer.

## 1. Question and theory

- What is the research question, word for word? Is it descriptive, relational, causal, exploratory or a design question?
- Which theory or prior finding motivates it? Trace each cited finding to the paper, not to the citing paper's summary.

## 2. Question-to-method fit

| Question type | Approaches that can answer it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Causal (does X change Y?) | Experimental; quasi-experimental with named controls; mixed with an experimental strand | Name the comparison and the assignment mechanism |
| Relational (is X associated with Y?) | Quantitative correlational; quasi-experimental | Association only |
| Descriptive (what happens, how often?) | Any, chosen for the construct | Sampling frame decides how far it describes |
| Exploratory (how and why?) | Qualitative; mixed; design-based | Produces mechanisms and hypotheses |
| Design (does this design work here, and why?) | Design-based research; mixed | Iterations in a real setting with documented changes (Design-Based Research Collective 2003) |

## 3. Sample and setting

- Who was eligible, who was recruited, who consented, who finished? Compute retention.
- Volunteer and convenience samples describe themselves. A camp, lab or online study is a different setting from a school classroom.
- Record level (K-12, higher education, workplace), ages, prior experience, access to devices, and language.

## 4. Measures and validity

- Does each measure target the construct the claim names? Task completion with a tool present measures performance with the tool, not independent skill.
- Self-report of learning is perception, not learning.
- Scoring: rubric, number of raters, agreement statistic, how disagreements were resolved.
- Post-tests that stay inside the trained tasks measure retention, not transfer.

## 5. Analysis and uncertainty

- Tests used, corrections for multiple comparisons, effect sizes with intervals.
- Exclusions applied after assignment (completers only, items dropped by progress) can unbalance groups.
- Non-significance is not equivalence; post hoc subgroups are hypotheses.

## 6. Threats

Selection bias, confounding (different materials, videos, instructors or time on task between groups), differential attrition, measurement validity, novelty and researcher effects, tool version drift, and transfer to other settings (Shadish, Cook and Campbell 2002, chapters 2-3).

## 7. Program evaluation (bcit-program-research-review)

Examine coherence (prerequisites and outcomes connect across courses), assessment validity (tasks measure the stated objectives), accessibility, participation (who enrolls, persists and completes) and evidence of learning (scored work, assessment results, observations). Keep four outcomes apart: business subject mastery, technology fluency, pedagogical competence, and occupational assessment readiness. Evidence for one does not establish another. Hand standards and credential mapping to edu-standards-alignment and attach its result.

## 8. Reporting

Report against the AERA reporting standards: problem formulation, design, sources of evidence, measurement and classification, analysis and interpretation, generalization, ethics, title and abstract (American Educational Research Association 2006).
