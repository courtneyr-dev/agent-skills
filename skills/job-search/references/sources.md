# Sources

Ranked by signal. Spend your scan budget top-down.

## 1. Company ATS boards — highest signal

`boards.greenhouse.io/<company>`, `jobs.lever.co/<company>`, `jobs.ashbyhq.com/<company>`,
`<company>.workable.com`. These are the live source of truth: current, complete for that employer,
and the URL survives. Build a target list of 20–50 employers you would actually join and scan them
directly every run. Aggregators are a lagging index of these pages.

## 2. Community channels

Slack and Discord where your field gathers. Roles get posted here first, often by the hiring
manager, and small companies frequently never post anywhere else.

## 3. Niche and specialist boards

Boards specific to your function or industry. Lower volume, much higher relevance than the general
aggregators, and less picked-over.

## 4. Direct recruiter email

Not a source you scan so much as one you must not miss. Someone has already decided you might fit.
Always surface these at the top of the report regardless of score.

## 5. General aggregators — lowest signal, highest volume

Useful for discovering employers you did not know about. Treat any hit as a lead to resolve back to
the company's ATS, not as the listing itself.

## Choosing what to configure

Start with ten ATS boards and one community. Add sources when a run feels thin, and remove any
source that has not produced a single qualifying role in a month — a scan that takes twenty minutes
gets skipped, and a skipped scan finds nothing at all.

## Coverage gaps

When a source fails — timeout, login wall, layout change, rate limit — record it by name in the
report. An unreported gap silently shrinks your search, and the failure mode is invisible: the
report still looks complete.
